# Mnemonic Rules v2

Format: `word → translation sound-alike`  
Two words only. All lowercase. No narrative. No filler.

---

## 1. Precise English Translation First

**Exact meaning.** The translation must match the full contextual meaning of the Italian word.  
`nel` = "in the", `del` = "of the" — not just "in" or "of".

**Target level B2–C1.** Use standard, common English words: "journey", "straight", "dark".  
Avoid archaic or overly obscure vocabulary.

---

## 2. Phonetic English Match (the "sound-alike")

**Concrete and familiar noun.** The sound-alike must be a well-known, concrete noun or a famous proper noun.  
Examples: Messi, Oscar, Smartie, Tuna.

**Phonetic proximity.** Prioritise natural English pronunciation over literal spelling.  
`cammin → coming`, `nel → nail`.

**Strict length cap.** Keep the sound-alike brief — ideally 1–2 syllables.  
Reject overly long words: ~~vitamin~~, ~~minion~~, ~~viaduct~~.  
Prefer: vital, mime, view.

**Valid English only.** The word must exist in common English or be a globally recognised proper noun.  
Reject non-words like ~~metzo~~, or obscure acronyms and slang.

---

## 3. Two-Word Composition and Natural Grammar

**Meaningful pair.** The translation and sound-alike must sit side by side and form a natural, sensible English phrase or adjective-noun combination.  
`straight director`, `dark oscar`, `lost smartie`, `vital life`.

**Grammatical flow.** Order the two words so they read naturally in English — switch the position of translation vs. sound-alike when necessary.  
`vital life` not ~~life vital~~. `era was` not ~~was era~~.

**Zero filler. No story.** Provide only the Italian prompt and the two-word English pairing separated by an arrow.  
`word → translation sound-alike` — nothing else.

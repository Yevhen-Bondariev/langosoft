# Mnemonic Rules for LangoSoft

Derived from iterative feedback during the LangoSoft session. Follow every rule before writing or approving a mnemonic.

---

## The Pattern

Every mnemonic has exactly **two CAPS words** in a short scene:

> **[CAPS phonetic anchor] + [CAPS translation] + minimal connecting scene**

- **CAPS phonetic anchor** — a common English word that *sounds like* the Italian word
- **CAPS translation** — the English meaning of the Italian word
- The scene is just background: a situation where the anchor produces or relates to the meaning

The two CAPS words are the mnemonic. The sentence is just glue.

**Good examples:**
> `acqua` → "A duck goes QUACK in WATER." (QUACK sounds like acqua, WATER is the meaning)
> `dolore` → "A DOLLAR gets stolen and deep GRIEF fills the heart." (DOLLAR sounds like dolore, GRIEF is the meaning)

---

## Rule 1: Anchor word must be a real, common English word

Target vocabulary: **B1–B2 level**. The user is not a native English speaker.

**Forbidden anchor words (examples of what was rejected):**
- Too obscure: AERIE, MOGUL, FILIAL, VEDIC, LACHRIMAL, PELAGIC, INTROIT, COMPUNCTION, BEATITUDE, ATTRITION, IMPIOUS, FIE, GENTRY, PERDITION, PLANGENT, SEMPITERNAL, PALLBEARERS, FURORE, SAPIENT

**Test:** Would a B1 English learner know this word immediately? If not, replace it.

---

## Rule 2: No fake phonetic spellings

The anchor must be a real word, never a transcription of sounds.

| ❌ Fake | ✓ Real |
|--------|--------|
| PAH-CHAY | PATCHES |
| TAH-CHAY | ATTACHED |
| AEH-reh | AIR |
| OO-SHEE-TOE | SHOO |
| VOL-ya | VOLCANO |
| DEE-CHESTY | CHEST |

Hyphenated pairs are only acceptable if **both sides are real English words** (e.g., ON-DAY, SIR-AH, PRE-ARRANGED).

---

## Rule 3: No foreign/Latin/French/Spanish anchor words

Even if the word exists in English dictionaries, avoid it if its origin is non-English and it is not commonly known.

| ❌ Forbidden | Why |
|-------------|-----|
| AQUA | Latin, not basic English |
| AMOR | Latin/Italian |
| ANIMA | Latin |
| DOLOR | Latin/Spanish |
| VOILÀ | French |
| ESPERANZA | Spanish |
| TRATTORIA | Italian |
| ALIUS | Latin |

**Exception:** Words genuinely absorbed into everyday English (INFERNO, PIANO, VOLCANO) are fine.

**Famous brand names and proper nouns are also allowed** as long as they are not circular (i.e., not the Italian word itself):

| ✓ Allowed | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| GUCCI | world-famous brand, instantly visual |
| FERRARI | same |
| MOSES | biblical figure everyone knows |
| FIAT | absorbed into English |

The banned DOLCE (for `dolce`) and AMORE (for `amore`) were rejected because they ARE the Italian word — not because they are brands or proper nouns.

---

## Rule 4: No circular mnemonics

Do not use the Italian word itself (or a near-identical form) as the English anchor.

| ❌ Circular | Problem |
|------------|---------|
| TUTTI-FRUITY for `tutti` | tutti is the Italian word |
| AMORE for `amore` | same word |
| DOLCE (D&G brand) for `dolce` | same word |
| AQUA for `acqua` | same word, Latin |

---

## Rule 5: Anchor word must actually sound like the Italian

Check Italian pronunciation rules before choosing an anchor:

| Italian spelling | Sound | ✓ Anchor examples | ❌ Wrong anchors |
|-----------------|-------|------------------|-----------------|
| c + e/i | "ch" (church) | CHERRY, CHEST, CHEETAH | CERTIFY (s-sound) |
| c + a/o/u | "k" | KEY, CAKE, KING | — |
| ch | "k" | KEY, PARK | CHEF (sh-sound) |
| g + e/i | "j" | JAY, JUDGE, LEDGER | GEAR (hard g) |
| gn | "ny" | CANYON, ONION | SIGN (silent g) |
| gli | "ly" | MILLION, LILY | — |
| sc + e/i | "sh" | SHELL, DISH | SCIENCE (s-sound) |
| h | silent | AH, OH | HA, HO (audible h) |

---

## Rule 6: No "X means Y" redundancy

Never explicitly state the connection. The scene should imply it.

| ❌ Redundant | ✓ Better |
|-------------|---------|
| "acqua is WATER" | "A duck goes QUACK in WATER." |
| "aere is AIR" | "Fresh AIR floats in the open sky and fills the ATMOSPHERE all around." |
| "JUSTICE — almost the same word: JUSTICE served" | "A JUDGE slams the gavel and JUSTICE gets delivered, fair and final." |

---

## Rule 7: If a word is an obvious cognate, skip or keep minimal

If the Italian word already sounds exactly like its English meaning, no mnemonic is needed or a one-line note suffices. Do not write a story explaining what is self-evident.

---

## Rule 8: The anchor must be a noun — and that noun should be the subject of the story

Nouns are the key. They give the story a subject, and a subject naturally demands an object and an action. Adjectives and verbs make weak anchors because they do not produce an actor.

**The structure to chase:**

> **[NOUN anchor as subject] + [action that earns the meaning] + [meaning as object or outcome]**

| ❌ Adjective/verb anchor | ✓ Noun anchor |
|------------------------|--------------|
| MISTY sadness fills the room | MESSI sits SAD after losing the World Cup |
| COZY comfort wraps her | a COZY chair wraps her ← still OK if chair is the image |
| FUGITIVE ordered to flee | a FUGU fish FLEES from the chef |
| MORE (for amor) | a BUCKET wanting MORE at the well |

**Why the noun anchor wins:**
- MESSI is instantly visual; MISTY is an adjective you have to imagine
- A KIWI standing still earns HERE; a QUIZ just labels it
- A FUGU fleeing earns FLEE; FUGITIVE just restates the meaning

**The anchor earns the meaning — it does not just sit next to it.** The noun's action or situation should make the meaning feel inevitable, not announced.

| ❌ Label | ✓ Earned |
|---------|---------|
| QUIZ appears HERE | a KIWI stands HERE and refuses to move |
| SEE SO and THUS | a SEE-SAW tips SO far that THUS the other side follows |
| SENSEI: SAINT | a SENSEI bows before the SAINT, both honored with the highest title |

---

## Rule 14: Choose universal nouns over niche proper nouns

A famous person is only useful if a B1 English speaker *anywhere in the world* recognises them instantly. When in doubt, replace a specific person with a common noun that carries the same feeling.

| ❌ Niche | ✓ Universal |
|---------|------------|
| AL GORE (politician, not global) | a LORD |
| a regional celebrity | a KING, SOLDIER, MONK |

Famous athletes, brands, and biblical figures with truly global recognition are fine (MESSI, MOSES, FERRARI, GUCCI).

---

## Rule 15: Phonetic looseness is acceptable when the noun is iconic

If a noun produces an irresistible image, a one-syllable phonetic match is enough — you do not need every syllable to line up perfectly.

| Italian | Loose but iconic | Why it works |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| dove (DOH-veh) | DOVE (the bird, "duhv") | the bird flying = WHERE instantly |
| san | SENSEI | title of reverence = SAINT by analogy |
| mesti | MESSI | first syllable matches; the image dominates |

This applies only when the noun is so vivid that the mnemonic is unforgettable. A weak noun with loose phonetics is still a bad mnemonic.

---

## Rule 9: Keep sentences short and simple

- Max 1–2 short sentences
- One clear scene
- No academic language, no multi-clause explanations
- Inflected base form in parentheses at start: `(cammino) A CAMEL...`

---

## Rule 10: Maximize syllable coverage

The anchor must match **as many syllables of the Italian word as possible**, not just the first one.

| Italian | ❌ Weak (1 syllable) | ✓ Better (2+ syllables) |
|---------|---------------------|------------------------|
| dolore (doh-LOR-eh) | DULL | DOLLAR |
| perduta (per-DOO-tah) | PURR | PRODUCE, PURDUE |
| lasciate (las-CHA-teh) | LASS | LATCH |

If you can only match one syllable, it's acceptable — but always search for a word that covers more first.

---

## Rule 11: The scene must be a real scenario with an actor and an action

"[ANCHOR] [TRANSLATION]" is not a mnemonic. The scene must show a situation where the anchor word produces, causes, or relates to the meaning.

Every mnemonic must answer: **who does what?** If you can't name an actor performing an action, rewrite it.

| ❌ Label only | ✓ Real scene |
|--------------|-------------|
| `DOLLY in pain: SAD / SORROWFUL` | `A DOLLY breaks and pain follows: DO LENT leaves the heart SAD and SORROWFUL.` |
| `FACTORY: MAKER` | `The FACTORY hums all day and IT MAKES and PRODUCES everything.` |
| `A DOLLAR stolen: PAIN` | `A DOLLAR gets stolen and deep GRIEF fills the heart with PAIN.` |

Gold standard: `città` → "A CHEETAH dashing through the CITY." — vivid action, both CAPS words visible, no redundancy.

---

## Rule 12: Two phonetic anchors are better than one when they cover the full word

When two common English words together cover all the syllables of the Italian word, use both as phonetic anchors — the scene connects them to the meaning.

| Italian | Syllables | Anchors | Scene |
|---------|-----------|---------|-------|
| dolente (doh-LEN-teh) | doh + lent | DO + LENT | `DO LENT together: the fast leaves the heart SAD and SORROWFUL.` |
| perduta (per-DOO-tah) | per + doo-tah | PEAR + DUTY | `A PEAR on DUTY vanishes without a trace, LOST from its post.` |
| dolore (doh-LOR-eh) | dol + or | DOLLAR | single word covers both syllables |

---

## Rule 13: No em-dashes, semicolons, label colons, or equal signs

All four are shortcuts that replace a story with a label. All four are banned.

| ❌ Banned form | Example |
|---------------|---------|
| Em-dash | `POET — verse-maker` |
| Semicolon | `SONAR detected; SOUND below` |
| Label colon | `SAVAGE: WILD and untamed` |
| Equal sign | `O = OR` |

| ❌ Half-baked | ✓ Story |
|--------------|--------|
| `PUNCTUAL arrival at the exact POINT / MOMENT.` | `A PUNCTUAL train hits the exact POINT and the crowd marks THAT MOMENT.` |
| `POET — verse-maker, a speaker of truth.` | `A POET climbs the cliff and shouts TRUTH into the wind below.` |
| `SAVAGE: WILD / SAVAGE and untamed.` | `A SAVAGE beast runs WILD and SAVAGE, completely untamed and free.` |
| `O = OR, one or the other.` | `She says OR pick this one, just one or the other, that is what OR means.` |

---

## Checklist before writing

- [ ] Anchor word is B1–B2 common English
- [ ] Anchor word is a real word (not a phonetic transcription)
- [ ] Anchor word is not Latin/French/Spanish/Italian
- [ ] Anchor word is not the Italian word itself
- [ ] Anchor word sounds like the Italian (check pronunciation rules above)
- [ ] Anchor word covers as many syllables as possible (not just the first)
- [ ] Anchor word is a **noun** — if not, find the noun version
- [ ] The noun anchor is the **subject** of the sentence
- [ ] The noun's action or situation **earns** the meaning — does not just label it
- [ ] No explicit "X means Y" statement
- [ ] Sentence is short (1–2 sentences max)
- [ ] Exactly two CAPS words minimum: one sounds like the Italian, one is the meaning (two phonetic anchors are OK when they cover the full word)
- [ ] No em-dashes, semicolons, label colons, or equal signs
- [ ] If using a famous person, they must be universally recognised (MESSI yes, AL GORE no)

---

## Revision checklist (when auditing existing mnemonics)

Flag and replace any entry where:

1. **The anchor is an adjective or verb** — MISTY, COZY, WEARY, FUGITIVE. Find the noun instead (MIST, COT, SOLDIER, FUGU).
2. **The anchor just sits next to the translation** — "SEE SO and THUS" vs "a SEE-SAW tips SO." The anchor must *do* something that makes the meaning feel like a consequence.
3. **The anchor is circular** — SAINTLY for SAINT, POETIC for POET. The anchor must be a different word from the meaning.
4. **The proper noun is niche** — AL GORE, regional politicians, local celebrities. Replace with a universal noun (LORD, MONK, SOLDIER) unless the person is truly global (MESSI, MOSES, FERRARI).
5. **Syllable coverage could be upgraded** — if the current anchor matches one syllable and a better noun covers two, swap it (QUIZ → KIWI for quivi, MISTY → MESSI for mesti).
